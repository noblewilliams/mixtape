Mix ready notifications: the ask

Founder ask (2026-11-21): "a modal for the request with cool tape illustration showing why we need it and a cta that makes the request". Shown once: to a new listener while their first prompt is processing (or on Home if they just signed up), and to an existing listener on Home if they have never been asked. Spec: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-21-durable-mix-turns-design.md.

What you are approving
  • The container: the app's standard sheet (MixtapeSheet: full width, 20 pt top corners, one handle, light scrim), the same one as "Before your first mix". It is modal: the listener taps a button or swipes it away. On the web it is the standard compact dialog (ControlModal), like the AI-consent dialog.
  • The art: I1, a "Your mix is ready" notification popping up out of the cassette while its hubs turn recommended, or I2, the cassette buzzing like a phone.
  • The copy: headline "Want a ping when it's ready?", one sentence of why, the paper pill "Turn on notifications" (the only thing that brings up iOS's own prompt), and "Not now".
  • The states: where it shows, what happens after Allow, Don't Allow and Not now, the You row, what actually arrives (including the "Oops" failure), 200 % text, reduced motion, dark, and web.
Recommendation: I1 in the standard sheet. The art shows exactly what they're agreeing to, which is the "why", so the words can stay to one sentence. I2 is cuter but explains less.
Not on this board: Android (no Android app yet). The quick fix's local notification needs no art of its own, since it uses the same "Your mix is ready" wording as the push.

1 · Where it shows

Never at launch, and never as iOS's own prompt cold. A flag is set the moment it appears, so it never shows twice on one install, whichever way it is closed. If iOS already knows the answer, it's skipped: already allowed means it quietly registers, already denied means it never shows.

6 · Web

The same art and words in the web app's compact dialog, using its quiet text buttons. It is offered only where the browser can actually receive push. On iPhone that means the app added to the Home Screen; in a Safari tab it never shows. The click on "Turn on notifications" is what lets the browser ask. "Once" is remembered in this browser.

To decide

  • Headline: "Want a ping when it's ready?" (drawn), or "Want a ping when it's pressed?", which is more on-theme but slightly less plain.
