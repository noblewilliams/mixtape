Mix conversation, tape and versions redesign

Answers the founder's device review of build 202611030734. It reworks five surfaces: the conversation transcript, the bottom panel, the tape (arrangement) screen, tape settings and version history. The frame from the shell approval stays: gradient glow, glass clusters, flush lists, SF only, cassette as the one motif. Song artwork is drawn as placeholder gradients. Names and messages are made up.

What this board changes
  1. Transcript reads like lyrics, at a calmer size Revision 2. No bubbles. The DJ's lines are large and bold on the left; your lines are smaller, grey and right-aligned. The latest exchange is at full strength; older turns fade back, like past lyric lines.
  2. One split Play button. Play now and Send to Music become one Play with an arrow. The main part plays wherever you last chose, starting with Mixtape's own player because that player learns from listening (decision of 9 September). The arrow offers Play here or Play in Apple Music, and the app remembers your pick. Nothing is deleted. Revision 2
  3. The bottom panel holds one tape and the composer. A strip for the current tape (cassette, name, version, Play) sits above the composer and stays there. Tap it to open the tape. + Playlist and Shape move behind one + button, and Shape only appears before the first mix. After that, you ask the DJ in words.
  4. Create playlist lives on the tape screen, next to Play, in a bar that stays put while you scroll.
  5. The header only holds Back and More. The tape screen puts its title under a large cassette, so it has room to wrap. The chat header drops the queue button because the strip does that job.
  6. Tape settings is an opaque sheet with real cassettes, sorted into eight colour families.
  7. Versions shows Now and Earlier sections, a readable time, and the message that made each version. The tick, the green "Current" label and the "may not have been saved" note are gone.
  8. Menus are popovers. The glass menu that drops from the button replaces the action sheets that slide up from the bottom, across the app. Shape on Home becomes one of them. Revision 2
  9. Smaller glass header buttons, app-wide. Back, More and every other top button becomes its own 34 pt liquid-glass circle (44 pt tap area), replacing the large white capsules. Revision 3

1 · Conversation

The latest DJ line and the one before it are at full ink. Earlier turns sit at about 34% opacity, and a turn comes back to full when you scroll it into the lower third (as lyrics do). Version markers replace the grey "Version 1" pill and the "The tape" card. They are a small cassette in the mix's own tape colour plus a single line, so they stand out without taking more room. The current version shows in the strip, so the transcript never repeats it as a card.

2 · The tape

The cassette, in the tape's colour, leads the page, and the title sits underneath where it can wrap to two lines. The navigation bar shows only Back and More until you scroll; after that, the small title fades into the space between them. Play and Create playlist sit in a bar at the bottom that stays put. The "Tap a song for its note" hint moves under the last song.

3 · Tape settings

A bottom sheet on the solid sheet surface replaces the see-through centred dialog. The 48 colours stay, sorted into the eight families the palette already has. One row of family swatches picks a family, and its six colours appear as real cassettes with their names. The selected one lifts and its hubs turn once. Rename sits at the top because it is the other thing people come here for.

4 · Versions

The title is "Versions" in the 28 pt large title. Now holds the current version and Earlier holds the rest, so no badge is needed. Each row shows a readable time and, as a second line, the message you sent that made the version. That message comes from the transcript the app already has, so the server is not involved. Restored versions say "Restored from version 1" instead. Tapping Now opens the tape; tapping an earlier version opens its preview.

5 · Menus Revision 2

There is one menu style: the glass popover anchored to the control that opened it. It replaces every action sheet that slides up from the bottom. This reverses the smoke round three choice (note 8) of action sheets over Material popups. That choice was about grey Material boxes clashing with glass; this popover is drawn in the sheet surface instead. Tapping outside closes it, so there is no Cancel row. Destructive entries are red and sit last, behind a separator. Toggle-style entries carry a tick. With reduced transparency, the menu is opaque.

Changing from action sheet to popover: conversation More, tape More, mix row long-press, Now Playing More, playlist detail and edit, What the DJ knows More, routine pill, attachment chip, the import review's "Set all files", and Shape (Home and conversation).
Staying as sheets, because they hold content rather than a list of actions: Keep this mix, the AI consent sheet, library sync, import, the playlist picker, the playlist chooser in import review, routine setup, and Now Playing's queue.

6 · Header buttons Revision 3

Every top-of-screen button in the app (Back, More, Version history, Sync, Close and the rest) becomes its own small circle of liquid glass. That replaces today's 44 pt white capsules, which group two buttons together. The circle is 34 pt, the glyph 17 pt, and the tap area stays 44 pt (dashed outline below). On iOS 26 and later it is the real UIKit glass the shell already hosts, which bends and tints whatever is behind it. On older iOS it is a frosted fallback, and with reduced transparency it is a solid fill. The title gets the width the capsules used to take.

What approval covers

  • iOS, light and dark, default and largest text sizes, for the conversation, tape, tape settings, versions, version preview, the app-wide menu presentation, and the app-wide header buttons. Reduced motion: the dots and hub turn are static, and the fade on older turns stays (it is not motion). Reduced transparency: the panel and sticky bars become opaque.
  • This supersedes the bubble turns, the in-transcript "The tape" card, the grey Version pill, the attachment row, and the three-action row in conversation states and the arrangement board. All other locked behaviour carries over (clarifications, attachment errors, working state, failures, opening skeletons).
  • Decided in revision 2: split Play (B). Still open: whether the older-turn fade should follow your scroll like lyrics, or stay a fixed "latest exchange bright" rule. The scroll version looks better but is a little more work and harder to test.
  • Not covered: web. Web keeps its rail until it gets its own board.
