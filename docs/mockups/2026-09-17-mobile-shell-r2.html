Native iOS · shell, motif and playback · revision 2 · synthetic data

Four tabs, a prism, and a boxy grid.

Revision 2 replaces the hub-and-sheet proposal after the founder's direction of September 17. The shape is now decided: Home, Mixes, Library, You on a native tab bar. This board shows how that shape looks with the reference's boxy layout, the prism gradient that comes off the cassette as the one signature motif, a gradient app background, Apple Music's large-title collapse and floating playback controls, and native Liquid Glass with a fallback for earlier iOS.

What changed from revision 1
  • Tab bar replaces the You sheet. Your music becomes the Library tab; the DJ's memory, listening, suggestions and Account live under You.
  • Home is reduced to the composer plus a list of ideas (routine suggestions and starter prompts), modelled on Apple's Playlist Playground sheet.
  • Cards, tiles, buttons and inputs are boxy: 2–4 point radii, hairline borders, light grey fills. Only native chrome (tab bar, mini-player) keeps the system's pill shape.
  • Display type moves from the handwritten marker to a serif, matching the reference. New York ships with iOS, so nothing is bundled. See question 1 at the end.
  • The prism replaces the tape-button styling: primary buttons, the composer stripe, progress, the focused field and the brand banner carry it. Nothing else does.

2 · The shell

Large title with Apple Music's top margin, collapsing on scroll into a small centred title over a progressive blur with no bottom edge. The tab bar is a floating glass pill that minimises to the active tab while scrolling, exactly as iOS 26 does natively; the mini-player rides above it and stays put.

3 · Playback controls

Look and behaviour follow Apple Music: a floating mini-player that expands into a full-height Now Playing sheet with artwork-derived colour behind a blur, a thick scrubber, three large transport controls, a volume slider, and a bottom action row. Our action row carries mix actions instead of lyrics, which the product never shows.

4 · Native glass and the fallback

On iOS 26 and later the tab bar and mini-player are the system's Liquid Glass: refraction, specular top edge, minimise-on-scroll. Earlier iOS gets a frosted pill with the same size, blur and radius, a hairline edge, and no refraction highlight. Both crops sit over the same busy artwork so the difference is honest.

Implementation constraint to decide (question 2). Flutter draws its own widgets, so "native Liquid Glass" means one of two things: host a real UIKit tab bar and mini-player in the iOS runner and bridge tab changes to Flutter, or draw a Flutter replica of the glass. Only the first is the genuine system material and gets Apple's future refinements for free; it costs a platform-view seam. The board draws the genuine material and the fallback; the choice changes engineering, not the look approved here.

5 · Foundation

The prism · one motif

The five label stripes on the cassette are the palette. The prism is those stripes with the plum end added, run diagonally. It is the only decorative colour in the app.

Where it appears: primary button fill, composer left stripe and focus ring, playback progress, the You avatar, the brand banner, and a suggestion's tile. Where it never appears: backgrounds behind text, borders on cards, tab bars, icons, or more than one element per screen.

Background · never flat

Light: warm off-white falling to a cooler grey, a blue tint top-left and a pink tint bottom-right at 10–14% alpha. Dark: near-black with the same two tints. Cards sit on top as translucent white or white-at-6%, so the gradient shows through the hairline gaps. Now Playing swaps the tints for the artwork's two dominant colours.

6 · Variants that change the decision

Reduced motion: hubs still, examples fixed on the first prompt, sheet and glass appear without slide, title collapse becomes a cross-fade. Reduced transparency: glass and the title blur become opaque system material; the gradient background stays because it is not transparency. Short phone (568 pt): Home shows the composer and two ideas before scrolling; the dock height is unchanged.

7 · Questions before approval

  1. Serif display. The reference uses a serif; I have drawn New York for large titles, small titles and mix titles, and retired Noteworthy except on the cassette label art. Keep the serif, or keep Apple Music's bold sans for titles and use the serif only for mix names?
  2. Native glass in Flutter. Genuine Liquid Glass requires hosting a UIKit tab bar and mini-player in the iOS runner and bridging to Flutter. A Flutter replica is cheaper but is not the system material. Which do you want engineered? The look on this board is the same either way.
  3. Home contents. The Playground reference has a mic and a beta note; we have neither voice input nor a beta programme, so the board omits them. Should the ideas list mix routine suggestions and starter prompts in one list, as drawn, or keep suggestions in their own group?
  4. Prism saturation. The reference's beam is more saturated than the cassette stripes. I kept the cassette's exact colours so the motif is honest to the asset. Say if you want a brighter prism for buttons and progress while the cassette keeps its own.
  5. Android. New York and Liquid Glass are iOS only. The fallback shown for older iOS is also what Android would get, with Roboto Serif as the display face. Fine for now?

What approval covers

The four-tab shape and what belongs in each tab; the large-title placement and its collapsed blurred state; the floating mini-player and tab bar with their minimise behaviour; the Now Playing layout; the boxy radii, hairlines and fills; the gradient background rule; the prism's allowed uses; the serif and sans type roles; the cassette and icon set. iOS light and dark, 390-pt width, with 200% text, reduced motion and reduced transparency as described. It does not approve the conversation, arrangement, import, sign-in or account screens; those follow as individual boards in the order recorded in revision 1.
